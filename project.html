Struggles with Interpreting Slope: a digital green reader

A handheld training aid that measures the slope of a putting green, takes the distance to the hole, and rotates a laser to show golfers the line to aim along.

The problem

In the United States, 44.13% of amateur golfers have 14.9+ handicaps and experience difficulties when reading the slopes of greens. High-handicap golfers average four to five three-putts per 18 holes. In the team's survey, only 14.7% of golfers were fully confident reading greens, and 88.9% had three-putted at least once in a 9-hole round.

The solution

The golfer enters the putt distance in feet on the device's buttons and OLED display. An accelerometer measures the slope of the green like a digital level. An Arduino combines slope and distance to calculate the ideal putt line, then turns a servo so a green laser shows the golfer where to aim. Everything is housed in a 3D-printed PLA shell and runs on AA batteries.
